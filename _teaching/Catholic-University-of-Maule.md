In this institution I did teaching in the following courses as a responsible professor:

Sistemas Dinámicos Lineales
======
This course focuses on the mathematical analysis of continuous and discrete linear systems. Students apply mathematical foundations—including Fourier series, Fourier transforms, Laplace transforms, and Z transforms—to analyze signals and dynamically represent systems. The curriculum explores graphical representation methods such as Bode and Polar/Nyquist diagrams, while introducing state variables, the linearization of continuous and discrete systems, and the core concepts of system stability, controllability, and observability.

Sistemas de Control I
======
This course explores the core foundations of automatic control systems. Students learn to analyze and design Single-Input Single-Output (SISO) control loops and classic PID controllers. The curriculum covers frequency domain design limitations, internal model principles, and the implementation of feedforward and cascade structures. Furthermore, the course delves into the parameterization of controllers, linear modeling in state space, and control design utilizing state variables.

Sistemas de Control II
======
Building upon Control Systems I, this advanced course introduces computer-based control systems, covering sampling, reconstruction, and analog-to-digital conversion. The curriculum emphasizes the analysis of discrete-time systems in the Z-plane, closed-loop stability, and state-space representations. Students design regulation and tracking loops via pole placement using polynomial and state-variable representations, and learn practical methods for approximating continuous controllers and implementing digital PID controllers.

Electromagnetismo
======
This theoretical-practical course introduces the foundational concepts of electricity and magnetism. Students learn to model and solve problems by applying fundamental laws, including Coulomb's, Gauss's, Ampere's, Biot-Savart, and Faraday's laws of induction. The comprehensive curriculum spans electric fields, potential, and capacitors, to magnetic fields, inductance, and RL/RLC circuits. It also covers notions of alternating current, including phasors, impedance, transformers, and electro-mechanical conversion in electrical machines.

Electrónica Industrial y Automatización
======
This course provides a comprehensive overview of the components, equipment, and automated systems utilized in modern industrial processes. Students gain conceptual frameworks and practical tools starting with semiconductor physics (diodes, transistors, thyristors) and digital logic circuits. The curriculum advances to parameter identification using the least squares method and Arduino integration. Key industrial topics include programmable logic controllers (PLCs) architecture and ladder programming, the application of resistive, capacitive, and inductive sensors, various industrial actuators, and an introduction to industrial robotics kinematics.
